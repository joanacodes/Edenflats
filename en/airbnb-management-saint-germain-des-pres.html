Letting a furnished home in Saint-Germain-des-Prés

The neighbourhood grows around the church of Saint-Germain-des-Prés, one of the oldest in Paris, and the cafés that made its name, the Café de Flore and Les Deux Magots. Between the Seine and the Luxembourg Gardens, Rue de Seine, Rue Jacob and Rue de Buci are lined with art galleries, publishers and antique dealers.

Guests look for an apartment with character, well kept, in an old or Haussmann-era building. Expectations are high: careful photos, spotless linen and an attentive welcome make the difference in reviews.

Regulations

Letting in Paris: the essentials

90 days a yearThe maximum for letting your main residence as furnished tourist accommodation in Paris, since 1 January 2025.
Registration numberThe property must be declared to the City; its number appears on every listing.
Change of useLetting a second home for short stays requires City authorisation, usually with compensation.
Landlord’s consentRenting? You need your landlord’s written consent to sublet.

These rules change: check them before letting on paris.fr (in French).
