What are your fees?

We offer two plans:

  • The guaranteed-income contract: based on your dates, your property and the average Airbnb occupancy rate, we calculate an income that is guaranteed and paid to you whether or not the apartment is booked. Our average margin is 15%.
  • The actual-income contract: we take a commission of 18% to 25% on the income your property generates, depending on the length of the rental. The more it is booked, the more you earn; if it is not booked, which is fairly rare, you receive nothing.

With actual income, we move nightly prices up or down with supply and demand on Airbnb to maximise bookings. You can set a minimum nightly price below which we will not go.

How do you calculate my income?

Your income depends on several factors: the property itself (address, number of beds…), the number of nights you want to entrust to us and the average Airbnb occupancy rate for that period. That rate is calculated by our dynamic pricing algorithm, from supply and demand on Airbnb. You then choose between a guaranteed income and your actual income.

How are the keys handled?

We look after the keys for the whole rental period. We need two sets: the day before the rental, you drop the first one at one of our partner cafés; the second stays in the apartment, on the kitchen worktop. During the rental, one set goes to our cleaning provider and the other to the guests.

If you prefer, we can also open remotely, with a smart lock or a key box.

Do you screen guests?

Yes. A dedicated team handles only guest screening, communication and management, so your property is let to people you can trust. We look at each guest’s history and reviews, and often talk with them to understand the purpose of their stay. An algorithm we developed also helps us select guests against specific criteria:

  • we ask for their age and the reason for the booking, insisting with guests who have no reviews;
  • we favour guests with positive reviews and verified profiles;
  • we make bookings easier for families;
  • an ID is required to book: every guest’s identity is checked.
What are the rules for Airbnb in France?

The rules depend on the town and on your situation. The main ones:

  • Main residence: you can let it as furnished tourist accommodation for up to 120 days a year. Towns can lower this cap: in Paris it has been 90 days since 1 January 2025.
  • Registration: in towns that require it, including Paris, the property must be declared to the town hall and its registration number shown on every listing.
  • Second home or buy-to-let: in Paris, the inner suburbs and other towns, letting it repeatedly for short stays generally requires a change-of-use authorisation.
  • Tenants: you need your landlord’s written consent to sublet.

These rules change: check the ones that apply to your property with your town hall or on service-public.gouv.fr (in French) before you start letting it.
